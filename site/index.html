Reversibility and the commitment horizon

Notes on two attempts to make an action-chunking robot policy react to disturbances. The first attempt measured zero successes and the reason turned out to be a bug, not a bad idea. The second attempt was built to be honest about that, and produced a clear negative result with a mechanism.

The one-paragraph version

An ACT policy predicts 100 actions at a time and executes them blind. We wanted it to commit for a shorter time when it was in trouble. To decide "in trouble", we measured reversibility — the chance the task still succeeds if you replan right now. Reversibility turned out to be measurable and predictable (ρ=0.69), but gating the horizon on it gained nothing, because success barely depends on the horizon at all above a threshold. Right signal, wrong actuator.
